## 2. Verified facts

### F1. US traffic fatality and crash rates (NHTSA, read at source)
Source: NHTSA National Center for Statistics and Analysis, "Summary of Motor Vehicle Traffic Crashes: 2022
Data", DOT HS 813 643, September 2024 (PDF text read in full).
- 2022: 42,514 people killed; fatality rate **1.33 per 100 million vehicle miles traveled (VMT)**; 2021: 1.38.
- 10-year range 2013-2022: low 1.08 (2014), high 1.38 (2021). 2013: 32,893 killed, 1.10.
- 2022 VMT: 3,196,191 million miles. Estimated 5,930,496 police-reported crashes; 2,382,771 injured; injury
  rate 75 per 100 million VMT.
- Rural/urban 2022: 17,283 fatalities (41%) rural, 25,023 (59%) urban, 208 not reported; FHWA says 32% of
  VMT was rural. Rural areas: 20% of population, 32% of miles, 41% of deaths.
- **Derived:** police-reported crashes per 100 million VMT, 2022 = 5,930,496 / 31,961.91 = **185.55**
  (the bench's all-miles human crash baseline; label "derived from NHTSA 2022").
- **Derived:** rural fatality rate about 17,283 / (0.32 x 31,961.91) = 1.69 per 100M VMT; urban about
  25,023 / (0.68 x 31,961.91) = 1.15. Approximate because the 32% share is rounded. This is the exposure
  mismatch in one line: the same drivers have a rate half again higher on rural miles.
- Caveat to teach: police-reported crashes undercount all crashes (RAND uses separate "reported" and
  "estimated total" rates). An AV fleet with full telemetry reports more of its minor crashes than a human
  population does, so a like-for-like crash comparison is contested; teach the disagreement.

### F2. RAND "Driving to Safety" (Kalra and Paddock 2016, RR-1478, read at source)
Read in full from a mirror of the RAND PDF (rand.org returned 403 to the fetcher; the copy hosted on the
IFIP WG 10.4 wiki is the RAND report itself). DOI 10.7249/RR1478.
- Human benchmarks used (2013): 1.09 fatalities, 77 reported injuries, 190 reported crashes per 100M miles;
  32,719 fatalities in 2013 (the report's figure; NHTSA's current table says 32,893, a later revision).
- Zero-failure demonstration (Eq. 2, n = ln(1 - C) / ln(R)): to show a fatality rate of at most 1.09 per
  100M miles at 95% confidence takes **275 million failure-free miles**, about 12.5 years for 100 vehicles
  driving 24 hours a day, 365 days a year at 25 mph.
- Precision: to estimate the fatality rate to within 20% at 95% confidence needs about 96 fatalities, which
  is about **8.8 billion miles** (8,811,009,174), about 400 years for that fleet.
- Superiority: to show with 95% confidence and 80% power that the rate is 20% better than 1.09 needs
  **11 billion miles** (text: 518 years; Table 1 says 500 years - the report disagrees with itself; quote
  "about 500 years"). 5% better: 215 billion miles, nearly 10,000 years.
- Injury and crash rows of Table 1: 3.9M / 125M / 161M miles (injuries), 1.6M / 51M / 65M miles (crashes).
- Conclusion (read at source): developers "cannot simply drive" their way to safety; test driving alone
  cannot provide sufficient evidence for fatalities and injuries; other methods (simulation, scenario
  testing, staged deployment) are needed. The report also notes that with fewer than 30 events an exact CI
  can be used (Ulm 1990) - the bench uses the exact form.
- **Derived, our number:** at the 2022 rate the same zero-event calculation is -ln(0.05) / 1.33e-8 =
  **225.2 million miles** (the bench prints this). Teach why it fell from 275M: the human rate rose.

### F3. Rule of three (reported) and the exact Poisson bound (standard maths)
- Hanley and Lippman-Hand (1983), "If nothing goes wrong, is everything all right? Interpreting zero
  numerators", JAMA 249(13):1743-1745: with 0 events in n trials, the 95% upper limit is about 3/n.
  **Reported** (abstract not read at source; the result itself is derivable: -ln(0.05) = 2.996).
- Exact Poisson upper bound for k observed events at confidence C (Garwood 1936 form):
  U = chi-square quantile(C, 2k + 2) / 2, equivalently the gamma quantile with shape k + 1. For k = 0 it is
  -ln(1 - C), the rule of three at 95%. Standard textbook result; the engine computes it exactly and the
  selfTest checks it.
- Directly standardised (exposure-matched) rate bound: Fay and Feuer (1997) gamma interval, Statistics in
  Medicine 16:791-801. **Reported** for the citation; the formula is implemented and checked by simulation
  in the reference script (coverage check).

### F4. SAE J3016 levels (read at source)
Sources: SAE International blog "SAE Levels of Driving Automation Refined for Clarity and International
Audience", May 3 2021; the SAE J3016 visual chart PDF (2021, freely distributable as-is with attribution).
- J3016_202104, "Taxonomy and Definitions for Terms Related to Driving Automation Systems for On-Road Motor
  Vehicles", now free; first launched 2014; Level 0 (no driving automation) to Level 5 (full driving
  automation). 2021 changes: clarity between Levels 3 and 4, remote assistance vs remote driving, "Driver
  Support Systems" name for Levels 1 and 2.
- Chart: Levels 0-2 "You are driving whenever these driver support features are engaged"; Levels 3-5 "You
  are not driving when these automated driving features are engaged". Level 3: "When the feature requests,
  you must drive". Levels 4-5 "will not require you to take over driving". Level 4 features drive "under
  limited conditions and will not operate unless all required conditions are met"; Level 5 "under all
  conditions".
- Teach honestly: a level describes a FEATURE while engaged, not a car or a company; the conditions a
  feature needs are its operational design domain (ODD, term from NHTSA SGO page, F5).
- Contested, teach as disagreement: when Level 4 is broadly available and whether Level 5 is reachable.
  No dates appear on any page.

### F5. NHTSA Standing General Order on crash reporting (read at source)
Source: nhtsa.gov/laws-regulations/standing-general-order-crash-reporting (read 2026-10-16; data through
September 15 2026).
- First issued 2021; amended 2021, 2023, 2025 (third amendment issued April 24, effective June 16 2025).
- ADS (SAE Levels 3-5) crash reportable if ADS was in use at any time within 30 seconds of the crash and the
  crash met damage or injury criteria; Level 2 ADAS reportable only for a vulnerable road user struck, a
  fatality, an air bag deployment, or hospital transport. "Crash reporting criteria are stricter for ADS".
- "Data regarding the number of crashes reported ... have not ... been normalized or adjusted by any measure
  of exposure, including operational design domains or vehicle miles traveled." Reporting entities are not
  required to report vehicles operated or distances traveled.
- Limitations listed: reports may be incomplete or unverified; the same crash may have multiple reports;
  ADS and Level 2 were sometimes misclassified in earlier versions; telemetry differs by entity.
- Teach: a count of reports is a numerator with no denominator. a4 is built on this sentence.

### F6. Disengagement reporting (California regulation, read at source)
Source: California Code of Regulations, Title 13, section 227.50 (Cornell LII copy).
- "Disengagement" means a deactivation of the autonomous mode when a failure of the autonomous technology is
  detected or when the safe operation of the vehicle requires the test driver to take manual control.
- Annual report must give monthly autonomous miles on public roads, the circumstances, the location type
  (interstate, freeway, highway, rural road, street, parking facility) and who initiated it (technology,
  test driver, remote operator, passenger).
- A 2025 DMV proposal amends Article 3.7 including 227.50; adoption not confirmed: say "as of the 2024
  text". Critics call the definition open to interpretation (reported, trade press; no names on pages).
- Teach: who decides to take over is the operator's policy, so disengagements per mile measure the policy
  as much as the car. This is the bench's anti-lever.

### F7. Importance sampling for AV evaluation (read at source, abstract)
Zhao, Lam, Peng, Bao, LeBlanc, Nobukawa and Pan, "Accelerated Evaluation of Automated Vehicles Safety in
Lane-Change Scenarios Based on Importance Sampling Techniques", IEEE Transactions on Intelligent
Transportation Systems 18(3):595-607, 2017 (online 2016), DOI 10.1109/TITS.2016.2582208; arXiv 1605.04965.
- Cut-ins modelled from the University of Michigan Safety Pilot Model Deployment data; statistics skewed
  toward riskier behaviour; cross-entropy method picks the skew; estimates reweighted to real-world rates.
- Reported acceleration 2,000 to 20,000 times: about 1,000 simulated miles expose the vehicle to challenges
  that take 2 to 20 million real miles to meet. Quote as "the authors report".

### F8. Safety standards (reported tier, concepts only, no numbers)
- UL 4600, Standard for Safety for the Evaluation of Autonomous Products, Edition 3 published 17 March 2023
  (adds autonomous trucking): goal-based, technology-agnostic; a safety case = structured claims, argument
  and evidence across the lifecycle; assumes operation without human supervision; uses safety performance
  indicators. Reported (UL Standards & Engagement news and catalogue text).
- ISO 26262 (road vehicles, functional safety): hazards from malfunctions of electrical/electronic systems.
  ISO 21448 (SOTIF, safety of the intended functionality): hazards with no fault at all, from functional
  insufficiencies of the specification or performance limits (a sensor that works as designed and still
  misses). Reported (iso.org returned 403; concept-level only).

### F9. A public multi-sensor drive log (read at source)
Caesar et al. 2019/2020, "nuScenes: A multimodal dataset for autonomous driving", arXiv 1903.11027 (PDF read).
- 1,000 scenes of about 20 s; 6 cameras at 12 Hz, 1 spinning 32-beam lidar at 20 Hz, 5 radars at 13 Hz;
  360-degree coverage.
- Synchronisation: a camera's exposure is triggered when the top lidar sweeps across the centre of that
  camera's field of view; the image timestamp is the exposure trigger time and the lidar timestamp is when
  the full rotation completes. "The 12 camera exposures are spread as evenly as possible across the 20 lidar
  scans, so not all lidar scans have a corresponding camera frame."
- Keyframes annotated at 2 Hz (23 classes, 8 attributes, cuboids x, y, z, w, l, h, yaw); the authors found
  2 Hz annotations robust to interpolation to 10-20 Hz; 40k keyframes; 1.4M camera images.
- Lidar range typically 50-150 m; radar to about 200 m (paper's own description).
- Used in p1 as the real-world anatomy (as "a public research dataset", the name allowed in a6 readings
  only; p1 says "a public multi-sensor research dataset, read at source" and names it in the coverage row
  citation). Licence is non-commercial for the data: pages never ship rows from it.
- Other datasets as a6 self-study readings only, named, never promoted: KITTI (Geiger et al. 2012),
  Waymo Open Dataset (Sun et al. 2020), Argoverse (Chang et al. 2019). Named only, not read at source.

## 6. Bench design (assets/av-live.js)

Constructed generator, seed 1016 (canon), fleet miles 150M (canon), 95% one-sided bounds, IS budget 2,000
simulated encounters per rare class, 200 replicates for the variance comparison.
- Lanehart road contexts (constructed): highway 55% of miles, true 60 crashes per 100M; urban day 30%, 150;
  night 10%, 200; rain 5%, 330. Human mix (constructed): 35 / 40 / 15 / 10%. Human context rates are
  constructed multiples (0.50 / 1.15 / 1.40 / 1.60) scaled so the human mix averages EXACTLY the derived NHTSA
  185.55 crashes per 100M (F1). Labelled on the widget: only the 185.55 and 1.33 are real.
- Lanehart true fatal rate 0.3 per 100M (constructed), drawn by Poisson.
- Rare scenario classes (constructed; encounters per 100M miles x failure probability per encounter): pedestrian
  steps out from behind a parked van 4,000 x 0.003 = 12; wrong-way vehicle on a divided road 300 x 0.02 = 6;
  debris in lane at highway speed 2,500 x 0.004 = 10; emergency vehicle crossing a junction 1,200 x 0.015 =
  18. True tail total 46 per 100M. Failure happens when a nominal Exp(1) severity exceeds t = -ln(p), so the
  truth is known exactly; IS draws from Exp(mean t) and reweights by t exp(-x + x/t).
- Ladder: (1) naive "zero fatal crashes" (2) exact Poisson bounds, fatal and all-crash on Lanehart's own mix
  (3) exposure-matched crash rate with the Fay-Feuer gamma bound (4) + importance-sampled rare-scenario rate
  (point + 1.645 SE, summed with the matched bound, a conservative choice). Verdicts: "proven better" if the
  upper bound is below the human rate; "not proven" if only the point is below; "worse" if the point is not.
- Anti-lever (constructed): 2,000 conflicts per 100M supervised miles, severity U(0,1); a takeover happens when
  severity exceeds the policy's threshold (cautious 0.50, standard 0.90, loose 0.985); a conflict above 0.97
  that is NOT taken over crashes with probability 0.25. Score "disengagements per 100,000 miles" vs true rate.
- Break button: drop the four rare-scenario slices from evaluation (the tail rate becomes 0).
- selfTest (6 checks on load): zero-event bound = -ln(1 - C); Poisson bound above the count with exactly 5% in
  the tail (k = 0..40); one-stratum Fay-Feuer = exact Poisson; matched rate uses the human mix; IS unbiased
  within 6% over 300 replicates; IS weights average 1. Faults: boundLowerTail fails 3 checks, isNoWeight 1,
  ownMix 1.
